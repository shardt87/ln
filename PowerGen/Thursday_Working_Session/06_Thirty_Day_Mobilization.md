## Day-90 success, pivot, and stop criteria

| Decision | Evidence by November 18 | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale the federated model** | At least two external customers across the immediate priorities validate the problem and Southwire role; at least one pursuit reaches an evidence-backed specification, approved-product, RFP, quote, paid pilot, or equivalent commercial gate; buying-scope routes are resolved without material account damage; product/capacity/economics owners support continued pursuit; functional participation is sustained | Expand the lighthouse set and formalize resources/governance; consider whether added authority is needed |
| **Continue with narrow changes** | Customer need is validated but progress is limited by solvable taxonomy, resource, proof, or route issues | Extend only the supported priorities with corrective actions and a new dated gate |
| **Pivot the portfolio or operating model** | Customer demand exists but the proposed plays, route, offer, or federated decision rights do not convert; another lane or structure has stronger evidence | Change priorities, offer boundary, route rules, or authority; do not scale unchanged |
| **Stop the pilot** | No credible customer validation; no supported product/economic fit; recurring unresolved channel/account harm; or functional leaders will not commit resources | Return activity to existing businesses and retain only validated account/project work |

A standalone vertical should be considered only if customer/commercial evidence is real and recurring cross-business decisions still cannot be made under the federated charter. Structure is an outcome of evidence, not the starting assumption.

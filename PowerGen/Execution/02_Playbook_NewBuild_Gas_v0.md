## 1. Customer problem hypothesis

Owners/IPPs building new gas capacity face time-to-power pressure driven by electrical scope
coordination: late or fragmented decisions on specifications, vendor lists, package boundaries, cable
schedules, and material logistics extend schedule and labor. *(Source: S10 pp.5-8 positioning; S02
leadership questions. Hypothesis until customer discovery validates it.)*

## 2. Southwire offer hypothesis

Early electrical-scope coordination within each participant's contractual responsibility: project
intelligence and discovery, electrical-scope mapping, product/supply planning, compatibility
engineering, prefab/kitting enablers, and installation-ready material flow. Explicitly **not**
engineering-of-record, EPC, or installation responsibility.

## 3. Who specifies, who buys (map per project — never assume)

| Role | Typical actor | Playbook rule |
|---|---|---|
| Influences early scope | Owner/developer, owner's engineer | Enter before specifications and vendor lists are fixed |
| Specifies | EPC/OE engineering | Name the EPC/OE specification lead per pursuit |
| Buys factory scope | OEM/skid builder (factory-wired packages) | Runs in the OEM playbook lane; link records under one project ID |
| Buys field scope | EPC, contractor, distributor, or owner-furnished | Confirm actual buyer with vendor list, one-lines, cable schedule before forecasting |
| Installs / accepts | Contractor; owner acceptance | Map per S12 decision-rights model |

## 4. Route by buying scope (guardrails, per D-06/D-07)

- Record route and revenue owner per defined buying scope **before** proposal.
- Direct is not the default; distribution where cuts/kits/credit/staging/logistics create value;
  agents create access under existing agreements only.
- Owner/OE influence does not determine who buys the field cable. No double counting across lanes.
- Preserve existing routes pending commercial-owner decisions; unresolved conflict escalates per charter.

## 6. Discovery standard (common PowerGen questions)

1. Project, stage, schedule, and next decision date; who decides what, when.
2. Electrical scope status: one-lines, vendor list, cable schedule, package boundaries, terminal drawings.
3. Factory-wired vs. owner-furnished vs. EPC-procured vs. contractor-installed boundary.
4. Time-to-power pain: where schedule, labor, or supply risk actually binds.
5. Current supply arrangements, routes, and incumbent relationships (respect existing agreements).
6. What evidence would make Southwire's coordination role valuable to this customer.

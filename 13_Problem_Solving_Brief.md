## 1. Executive Summary (The One-Page Memo)

**Recommendation: Authorize the 90-day federated PowerGen pilot on Thursday, August 20 — organize the opportunity now; reorganize only if 90 days of evidence proves it necessary.**

- **The governing thought:** The Power Generation opportunity is real enough to organize and not yet proven enough to reorganize. Southwire should buy 90 days of evidence (August 21 – November 18) with a pilot that changes *decision-making* without changing *ownership*.
- **Three pillars support the recommendation:**
  1. **Only two of five opportunity lanes clear the validation bar today** — new-build gas project capture (weighted 3.50/5) and OEM/modular platform embedding (3.35/5). A narrow start protects credibility and scarce functional time.
  2. **A federated platform is the only operating model the current evidence supports** — it beats business-unit-led coordination on accountability and beats a standalone vertical on disruption, channel risk, and evidence burden. All existing commercial and P&L authority stays where it is.
  3. **Evidence discipline is the pilot's actual product** — reconciled figures, signed baselines, and complete pursuit charters are the currency of both decision gates: Amber's September ratify/modify/stop checkpoint and the November 18 scale/pivot/continue/stop review.
- **The ask:** Marc decides the ten register items D-01 to D-10 on Thursday; delegates are named August 21; no new headcount, systems, or budget — the investment is committed time from named functions.
- **The safeguard:** Pre-agreed exit criteria. If discovery disproves the proposition, resources cannot be committed, or channel commitments block the lighthouse pursuits, the pilot stops and activity returns to the businesses. Structure follows evidence, never the reverse.

## 2. SCQ Context

**Situation.** Southwire has genuine Power Generation momentum on several independent fronts:

- A developed OEM target model: **~$1.8B directional** annual U.S. non-turbine OEM wire-and-cable market, 55 priorities, 31 Tier 1 candidates, $9.7M quote activity *pending reconciliation* (S04, S09).

**Complication.** The activity is not yet one operating system, and the gap is now a decision-forcing problem:

- **No source establishes enterprise authority** over accounts, pricing, capacity, channels, or revenue — the working brief is a discussion draft, not a mandate.
- **The evidence base conflicts with itself:** twelve recorded conflicts (C01–C12), including a superseded $12.575B market estimate versus the corrected ~$1.8B directional model, and >$20M versus $9.7M quote activity.

**Question.** *How should Southwire organize and validate the enterprise Power Generation opportunity so that it can capture what is real — without damaging existing businesses, channels, or credibility — and what must be true by November 18 to justify scaling?*

## 3. Diagnostic Issue Tree (MECE Breakdown)

The core question decomposes into three mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive branches: the **external opportunity** (is the prize real, and where?), the **competitive capture mechanism** (can Southwire win it?), and the **internal organization** (how should decisions be made?). Evidence discipline is not a fourth branch — it is the standard every branch must meet to count as answered.

### Branch A — Opportunity: Is the prize real, and which lanes come first?

> **Governing thought:** The prize is directionally large but only two lanes have enough evidence to justify commercial validation today; the rest are mapped, gated, or deferred.

|---|---|---|
| Market size credibility | ~$1.8B OEM model and IPP planning figures survive reconciliation as directional planning anchors | Metric dictionary and sizing reconciliation with Finance signoff (target Sept 4); quote-level export resolving $9.7M vs. >$20M |

*Framework applied:* portfolio force-ranking with veto gates (a disciplined variant of market attractiveness screening); 3Cs for each lane (customer demand, Southwire capability, competitive/channel position).

### Branch B — Right to Win: Can Southwire convert influence into purchases without channel damage?

> **Governing thought:** Southwire wins by entering before specifications are fixed and running OEM approval and field conversion as two linked lanes — and this is safe only if route and credit rules are decided before the first proposal.

| Sub-issue | Hypothesis | Evidence required to prove/disprove |
|---|---|---|
| Capability credibility | Engineering depth (VFD/motor, hazardous-area, shielding, termination) — not access — earns the specification seat | Product/application engineering delegate validation of fit, qualification, and technical risk per pursuit |

*Framework applied:* value-chain and route-economics analysis — the commercial boundary (factory-wired / owner-furnished / EPC-procured / contractor-installed / distributor-served) determines offer, buyer, route, and revenue path; influence and purchase are linked by one project ID and never double-counted.

### Branch C — Organizing Model: What construct and decision rights maximize capture at minimum disruption?

> **Governing thought:** The federated platform dominates both alternatives on today's evidence — and the pilot itself is the test of whether it holds.

## 4. Strategic Recommendations (Pyramid Structured)

**The Answer: Authorize the 90-day federated pilot Thursday, with the narrow two-lane portfolio and the evidence guardrail in force from day one.**

### Pillar 1 — Only two lanes clear the bar today; starting narrow is the strategy, not a compromise

- **Validate now:** new-build gas project capture (3.50/5 weighted) and OEM/modular platform embedding (3.35/5) — the only lanes with both a developed target base and a testable route to commercial evidence.
- **Gate, don't chase:** repower/brownfield (2.75) activates only with a named sponsor, a named project, and a repeatable hypothesis; Data Center/BTM (2.60) and nuclear/SMR (2.10) are evidence-mapped only.
- **Two structural clarifications prevent scope creep:** EPC/OE specification is a cross-cutting *route* serving both priorities, not a separate market; prefab, kitting, and application engineering are *enabling capabilities*, not standalone plays.
- **Why it matters to the board:** every lane added before its evidence exists dilutes the same scarce functional hours the scorecard depends on — and hands skeptics a legitimate reason to stop the pilot in September.

### Pillar 2 — The federated pilot changes decision-making without changing ownership, which neutralizes the channel-conflict risk leadership has flagged

- **BU-led coordination is rejected** because it preserves exactly the problems on the table: fragmented visibility, weak enterprise accountability, and channel-conflict risk from fragmented rules.
- **A standalone vertical is premature:** maximum structural clarity at maximum disruption, resource need, and P&L conflict — with insufficient evidence today. It returns to the table only if the pilot proves both real commercial evidence *and* a persistent inability to decide cross-business questions under the federated charter.
- **The federated charter is explicitly bounded:** PowerGen owns the consolidated view, discovery standards, pursuit orchestration, escalation, and reporting. It may not reassign accounts, quote or approve pricing, promise capacity, change any route, or take P&L or revenue. Businesses keep every authority they hold today.
- **Route guardrails make it operational:** one project, one pursuit charter; route and revenue owner recorded before proposal; direct never the default; no uncoordinated competing quote paths; conflicts to the sponsor within the proposed 48-hour target (D-07).

### Pillar 3 — Evidence discipline is the pilot's product: both decision gates are bought with reconciled figures, not conviction

- **The guardrail freezes every unreconciled figure from executive and external use now** — the ~$1.8B model, the $53M potential, both quote totals, the superseded $12.575B, and the labor-hour case — until Finance signs the reconciliation (target September 4). Internally, every use carries "directional" plus its source.
- **Funnel counts are stage counts, never pipeline:** 500+/29/17 are stage-specific; Rev89C rows are modeled candidates. Presenting either as "active opportunities" is the credibility failure the pilot exists to end.
- **The 30-day scorecard is binary and dated:** 3–5 chartered IPP pursuits, 3–5 qualified OEM accounts, complete influence-and-buying maps, all nine RIDs decided or escalated, ≥1 externally approved proof point, ≥3 external discovery conversations, active named delegates.
- **The two gates are pre-agreed, which is what makes stopping cheap:** Amber's September checkpoint ratifies, modifies, or stops on a written evidence package; November 18 decides scale, continue-with-changes, pivot, or stop against criteria fixed before the pilot starts — at least two external customers validating the problem and Southwire's role, at least one pursuit at an evidence-backed commercial gate, routes resolved without account damage, and sustained functional support.

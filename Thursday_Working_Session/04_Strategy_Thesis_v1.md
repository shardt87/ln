## The thesis in one paragraph

Southwire takes ownership of one enterprise PowerGen strategy, written by Stephan and Marc in 30 days on Donna's request. The strategy has two parts: organize where Southwire already plays (Utility as the legacy vertical, Renewables as its own vertical, both incorporated into the overarching PowerGen story) and define strategy where none exists (the IPP space with modular power inside it, nuclear watched as new technology above). The full go-to-market strategy builds over three to four months in phases, but tranches mobilize immediately, IPP first, because outreach is already producing: one quote under NDA, one warm SVP engagement, one leave-behind in an IPP's hands, and three named targets sequenced.

## How Southwire wins (the argument that is already in market, T pp.2-3)

1. **Name the customer's clock.** Time to power is an electrical execution problem. Three execution risks carry the story: labor demand pressure, material and installation lead, fragmented coordination.
2. **Argue economics, not cable.** The levelized-cost-of-energy formula: energy delivered is the denominator, and conductor temperature rating changes deliverable amps. Southwire is a small share of capex with a high execution coefficient: "I know we are one percent, but if we fail, it could cost much more."
3. **Prove carefully.** The 4,400-to-~880 labor-hours case (Nedra's case study, Bloom Energy basis) is presented as adjacent evidence with a per-project scoping caveat, never a guarantee.
4. **Industrialize the site.** Prefab, kitting, and the prefab-yard-near-site concept cut journeyman and apprentice hours on site and de-risk testing; potentially a business in its own right.
5. **Kill change orders.** Rework and change orders are the biggest bleed in an IPP's budget; early material planning and coordination is the antidote.
6. **Offer the hedge, never the bypass.** Where direct purchasing comes up: the owner buys material direct, the EPC's electrical contractor installs. Cost certainty for the owner, no displaced partner.
7. **Enter through people.** A champion with a real relationship forwards the leave-behind and defends the case internally; a 30-minute call precedes any meeting; the visit is one hour, experts present, no sales deck.

## Route to market (rules in force, T pp.3, 7)

- Distribution and EPC relationships are load-bearing; nothing in the outreach signals bypass.
- OEM accounts are routed by how they already buy: big accounts through distribution; direct only where direct is the account's existing behavior (Powell as the example).
- The pitch material never states a direct offering; openness to discussion is the posture if the customer raises it.

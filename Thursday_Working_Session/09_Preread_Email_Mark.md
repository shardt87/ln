Subject: Thursday pre-read: Power Generation strategy spine and the ten decisions

Mark,

Ahead of Thursday morning in Carrollton. Everything below is expanded in the attached working pack; this note is the five-minute version. My aim for the session: we leave with the spine agreed, four plays resourced, and the September leadership update locked.

Where we actually stand. We have real activity but no enterprise picture. Utility and Renewables carry an established base. In IPP gas, NRG has our outreach and owes us feedback, Southern Power is a relationship awaiting a discovery call, CPV is a target. OEM outreach has a named owner and is deliberately paused until the message, the 90-day plan and the target map pass quality review. Repower, prefab and nuclear are concepts without a baseline behind them. No account anywhere is past Engaged. Our one outcome proof, the labor-hour result, is unverified until Nedra closes it out. None of that is bad news; it tells us the first problem is visibility and orchestration, not capability.

The thesis I want to test with you. Southwire becomes the electrical execution partner for generation projects and operating fleets: in early, before the bill of material is fixed, integrating specification, capacity planning, prefabrication and material management so the customer gets faster time to power and lower construction risk. Portfolio: defend and organize Utility and Renewables, accelerate IPP gas and EPC/OE specification, build OEM/modular and prefab into repeatable platforms behind their quality gates, incubate nuclear with a watching brief only.

The operating model I recommend. A federated horizontal platform, run inside the current structure. PowerGen owns the market thesis, portfolio priorities, one consolidated pipeline view, the executive narrative and pursuit orchestration. The businesses keep accounts, execution, pricing, manufacturing, channels and every dollar of booked revenue. I compared this against business-unit-led coordination and a standalone vertical; it is the only option that answers the mandate without a reorganization and without taking anything from anyone. Two-quarter trial, then we review with evidence. The comparison is attachment 03.

The route-to-market principle we need to adopt as policy, not leave as prose: owner engagement creates influence, not a transaction. Supply flows through whatever route the customer already uses, and every pursuit declares its route at qualification, before distribution or field resources engage. We are talking to owners today with no rulebook; the first real opportunity will surface a channel conflict unless this is settled Thursday.

Ten decisions are teed up in attachment 05, each with a proposed default so we ratify or amend rather than start from blank. The three I need most from you: confirm the mandate in writing, who asked for company-wide ownership and who owns it; agree the scope boundary with Data Center on behind-the-meter generation; and mandate the five businesses to feed the current-state baseline by August 28, because the September update stands or falls on it.

Two housekeeping items: tell me if an earlier version of my brief reached you so we work from one text, and I need your final attendee list for the September update before Amber releases the series.

The pack: 00 source inventory, 01 evidence ledger, 02 current-state map (this is the baseline template), 03 operating model options, 04 strategy thesis, 05 decision workshop, 06 thirty-day plan, 07 open questions. Attachment 04 is the one to read if you only read one.

See you Thursday at 9.

Stephan

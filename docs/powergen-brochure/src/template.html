03 / DEFINITION & SCOPE
Five verticals, one site, no tug of war.
What each vertical leads, the typical package it buys, and the boundary the room still has to confirm.
Power generation: any entity that generates or stores electricity and delivers it to grid operators or end consumers.

Conventional, renewable, nuclear and battery. Target audiences: generation asset owners and operators, design firms, EPCs, and generation equipment and component manufacturers. Initial focus: gas generation, engines, fuel cells and modular packages.

Regardless of what it sits next to. A gas turbine or fuel cell behind a data-center meter is a PowerGen application.

Open boundaryStorage

The BESS boundary with the Renewables and Energy Storage team is still open. Standalone storage needs separate ownership.

Shared platformsOne OEM lead per account

05 / WHERE CABLE LIVES
Four families run the whole site. Two scopes buy them.
Cable is in every zone, under one percent of plant cost, at the end of every contract branch. That is why the play is the specification and the package, not the reel.
MV powerPlant feeders and auxiliary systems
LV powerTray cable, motors, panels and equipment
Control & instrumentationSignals, monitoring and protection
GroundingBonding and the ground grid

Inside the package, between the packages

The OEM may supply wiring inside a package up to terminal boxes or local panels. The EPC or an assigned installer supplies the interconnects between packages. The exact terminal point follows the contract and the scope split: two cable scopes, two buyers, two specifications.

Reach the specification before it is written. Engineering tier or package first; the PO follows.

11 / SOLUTIONS & VALUE
The value proposition is the moat, not what sits inside it.
Candidates by lane, each with the product underneath and the evidence we have or still need. Presented for the room's view, not a sign-off.

OEM / EP&ES

  • Repeatable designs, fewer assembly hours. Standard specifications and qualified assemblies shipped as a finished EP&ES solution.
  • Products ahead of the trend. A 2.4 kV rating on standard cable ahead of 800 VDC, so customers do not jump to 5 kV.
  • A direct path when it qualifies. Jeff's team vets direct; distribution otherwise.

Evidence$9.7M quoted in July; Enchanted Rock $2.3M at ~40% quoted margin, order status to confirm.

EPC

  • Less field work. Factory-built, tested cable spine and terminations instead of site labor.
  • Material readiness. Standard put-ups, kitting, labeling and staged deliveries, run with distribution (CIE), not around it.
  • Engineering before the spec. The plant model and the one-line read: we reach the specification before it is written.

EvidenceCIE, Sept 2: fit, put-ups, visibility and delivery are what they get paid for; $24–28M directional across four Gemma opportunities. Fuel-cell spine ~80% less field labor, to confirm.

IOU / utility gen.

  • Predictable electrical delivery. Early planning and engineering support around the outage and energization dates.
  • The product the spec asks for. Thermoset CPE medium-voltage cable where the utility specifies it; HV underground and grounding where the project calls for it.

EvidenceTBD. Say it plainly; thermoset CPE is the lesson.

IPP / owner

  • Lower exposure at the electrical milestones. Owner requirements aligned with the EPC and OEM packages before procurement locks.
  • A leave-behind their engineers use. The plant model as the door-opener; the spine as the primary opportunity.

EvidenceNRG leave-behind forwarded to their generation team; Southern Power came to us.

Reduced installation timeCustom prefab and standardized designs across projects.
Material readinessReel IDs, kits and sequenced delivery.
Supply certaintyForecasts tied to a confirmed supply plan.
Execution speedEarlier decisions and repeat designs.
Measure hours, shortages, on-time delivery and electrical milestone dates. That is the answer to "prove it".

12 / LIFE-CYCLE FRICTION
Where cable causes friction, and what already takes it out.
Problem shares from Southwire's own publications. Most of the fixes already exist, built for data centers, utilities and contractors. What is missing is pointing them at gas plants before the spec is frozen.
Specify · exists

CableTechSupport, engineering letters, pre-planning calls. Proposed: the One-Line Read, reserved MV and HV production slots.

Procure · install · exists

SPEED Services, SIMpull and MAXIS, Contractor Solutions Professionals, kitting and labeling. Proposed: the factory-built spine, 4,400 to ~880 field hours.

Commission · operate · exists

Pre-energization guidance, nitrogen purge ($185K saved on one job), rejuvenation, resiliency assessments. Proposed: tray and duct room for a future carbon-capture island.

Houston is the first test: the problem, its current cost, repeatability, willingness to pay. Figures current before customer use.

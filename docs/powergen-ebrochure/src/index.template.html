01 / The electrical execution challenge

Cable is the last thing on every branch and the first thing on the schedule.

Cable selection, equipment interfaces, construction sequencing and energization are one chain. A specification written before the interfaces are agreed, a reel that arrives out of sequence, or a drive output matched to the wrong cable each costs the same thing: time to power.

Time to power

Reach the specification before it is frozen, with the plant model, the one-line and the cable schedule in the same conversation. Capability: CableTechSupport application review; Construction Planning Services takeoffs and pull calculations.

Installation labor and constructability

Reel lengths, pull sections, circuit identification and staged issue planned around the installation sequence. Capability: Contractor Solutions and SIMpull equipment planning; qualified prefabrication to an agreed engineered scope.

Material availability, coordination and traceability

Published constructions with specification numbers, kitting and labels by circuit, reel tracking and staged delivery. Capability: SPEED Services, Project Services, Southwire Reel Tracking System.

Reliable operation and lifecycle support

Constructions rated for the duty, and assessment or rejuvenation of existing feeders where a brownfield expansion depends on them. Capability: Field Assessment Services and cable rejuvenation, with published utility results.

03 / The cable offering

Seven families cover the plant's electrical scope.

Published references connect circuit requirements to specific constructions. Each family lists its core references first and the expanded families from the September 2026 offering after them; the catalog links every one to the manufacturer's document.

OEM internal wiring vs. field interconnection. The OEM wires inside the package to terminal boxes or local panels; the EPC or installer connects between packages. The terminal point follows the contract.
Fixed wiring vs. portable cable. DLO and other flexible constructions are portable-cable applications; they are not substitutes for fixed tray-rated wiring.
MV insulation level. 100% or 133% insulation level, voltage class and shield type are project selections from the one-line, not from the model.
VFD duty. Drive-output cable is matched to the drive system; qualify the drive before selecting the cable.
Hazardous locations. ARMOR-X MC-HL constructions, glands and seals follow the area classification and the complete installation.
BESS DC vs. solar PV. RenewaFLEX is approved internal BESS wiring; it is not a photovoltaic application. Immersion and circuit-integrity requirements are separate qualifications.

04 / From specification to installation

Make the schedule work in the field.

Coordinate cable, equipment interfaces and the installation sequence before material reaches the jobsite. Package the delivery around the pull sequence: reel lengths, circuit identification, staging.

Prefabricated cable spine, project development. A factory-built spine needs defined interfaces, a qualified assembly provider and an agreed test plan. Evaluate a pilot using factory labor, field labor, logistics, schedule and total installed cost. Availability and performance are project-specific.

From scope to release

Turn the cable schedule into a field plan.

Use one circuit record from specification through energization. Select the Southwire support scope with the project team.

Extend the lifecycle offer

Assess. Repair. Rejuvenate. Replace.

Choose the intervention from condition, duty and the outage window. Agree eligibility, acceptance and scope before release.

Service availability, repair suitability, testing and warranty are confirmed for the actual asset. Historical case results do not establish future performance.

05 / Beyond one plant configuration

Build the package around the application.

The combined-cycle model is a starting point. Adapt scope to the generation technology, site and operating duty. Do not combine plant configurations or carry the model's preliminary quantities into procurement.

Generation scope

Generation assets and the electrical balance of plant, up to the generator step-up transformer. Includes behind-the-meter generation packages wherever they sit.

Downstream facility scope

Campus distribution, UPS, PDU and the consuming infrastructure belong to the facility. Confirm the generation-to-campus handoff at the equipment boundary.

Grid interface

The GSU, point of interconnection, asset owner and route are agreed before the connection cable is selected. Underground and overhead connections need different engineering packages.
